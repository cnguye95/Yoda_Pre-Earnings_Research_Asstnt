# Judge justifications (examples)

## filing_extraction

- **DIS / baseline — 3/5:** Captures consolidated revenue and EPS, all three segments' operating income, major spending and repurchase guidance, and important updated carriage and FCC risks. However, it omits Entertainment and Sports revenue, consolidated net income, the completed NFL acquisition and resulting ownership changes, and the announced FY27 segment-reporting change. No post-filing 8-Ks were supplied.
  - > Q3 segment operating income: Experiences $3,017M (+20%), Entertainment $1,680M (+64%), Sports $858M (-17%). (report)
  - > Following the NFL Transaction, the Company has an effective 72% interest in ESPN and Hearst Corporation has an 18% interest. (evidence)
  - > The Company announced that much of its Consumer Products business, which is reported as part of the Experiences segment, will be brought under the leadership (evidence)
  - > We will report under the new structure commencing with our fiscal 2027 reporting, at which time we will have implemented changes to our financial reporting processes. (evidence)

## figure_accuracy

- **DIS / baseline — 4/5:** Nearly all checkable reported, consensus, market and calculated figures match, including prior-year Q4 segment income derived by subtraction. A minor financial-attribution error describes the $1.56 prior-year EPS contribution as a Hulu tax benefit: the source identifies it as the net Hulu Transaction Impacts, including the acquisition payment charged through noncontrolling interests. The $5.56B segment-income total correctly rounds the filing's $5,555M, despite the fact sheet displaying $5.55B. Analyst forecast ranges are not treated as factual errors.
  - > Prior-year Q3 carried a net +$1.31 of items, including a $1.56 Hulu tax benefit. (report)
  - > •A $3,277 million non-cash tax benefit recognized upon a change in Hulu’s U.S. income tax classification recognized in “Income taxes” and $477 million Hulu Charge recognized in “Net income attributable to noncontrolling interest” (Hulu Transaction Impacts) (evidence)
  - > Consensus EPS: $1.70 (evidence)
  - > Q4 FY25 Sports OI was $911M (derived from $2,882M for FY25 less $1,971M for 9M). (report)

## reasoning_quality

- **DIS / baseline — 4/5:** Generally strong conditional reasoning: identifies the extra week, questions the EPS comparison basis, explicitly flags ambiguous Sports guidance, and reconciles changed information. The beatable-EPS conclusion is less secure because it extrapolates four historical surprises and compares an unknown-basis consensus with an analyst-adjusted sequential run-rate. The stated Experiences guidance range also depends on an unstated numerical interpretation of qualitative guidance. These limitations stop it short of fully evidence-grounded conclusions throughout.
  - > The +132% implied growth compares a likely adjusted consensus with a GAAP base, so it overstates the real step-up. (report)
  - > A beat is also supported by the 4-for-4 record, and applying the 5.5% average surprise gives about $1.79 (my arithmetic). (report)
  - > The metric behind 'growing' is unclear and needs clarifying. (report)
  - > First and foremost, it's worth highlighting that the Sports segment today is growing when you look at the guidance that we have for the year, which excludes the 53rd week. (evidence)
  - > The size of the 53rd week in revenue, segment OI and EPS is not disclosed in any provided source. (report)

## evidence_labelling

- **DIS / baseline — 4/5:** Market information has named providers and as-of dates; news is dated and separated from filing results; the unknown call date and unavailable whisper are expressly disclosed. However, some claims tagged as guidance contain the analyst's numerical interpretation rather than management's stated outlook. The sources are usually recoverable, but the distinction between actual guidance and analyst assumptions could be clearer.
  - > FY25 Experiences OI was $9,995M and the 9M FY25 figure was $8,117M, so Q4 FY25 was $1,878M (derived). Guidance of the high end of high-single-digit full-year growth, excluding the 53rd week, implies FY26 OI of about $10.8-11.0B. (report)
  - > On 2026-10-28 the LA Times reported Disney is laying off 100-200 people in the Experiences division, stemming from a regular evaluation of priorities, investments and project timelines, per an unnamed source. (report)
  - > The transcript is undated. Context ('earnings release and Form 10-Q were issued earlier this morning') suggests it was held on the day of the 10-Q filing, 2026-08-05. (report)
  - > Whisper EPS: N/A — not looked up: next report is 17 days away (looked up automatically within 3 days). (evidence)

## source_traceability

- **DIS / baseline — 3/5:** Most claims carry existing evidence IDs and the sources dictionary maps them to locations. Several citations are adjacent to, rather than inclusive of, the supporting fact: Q.106 does not contain the repurchase target, Q.020 does not contain Experiences revenue, and the buyback-funding explanation requires C.024 rather than the cited C.023. The $959M year-to-date impairment cited to Q.065 is stated precisely in Q.074 instead. These are localized but repeated traceability gaps.
  - > The buyback target rose to at least $9B from about $7B, largely because cash set aside for the OpenAI deal was freed up and A+E sale proceeds are expected. The 10-K had targeted $7B. (report)
  - > See Note 10 to the Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements for a summary of dividends and share repurchases. The Company is targeting at least $9 billion in share repurchases in fiscal 2026. (evidence)
  - > And the reason we're doing that is largely to utilize the cash that had been set aside previously for the OpenAI deal and now from the expected proceeds from the A&E transaction, which was announced overnight. (evidence)
  - > A+E impairments totaled $959M year to date, and closing is expected by fiscal year-end, which affects Q4 GAAP and the buyback funding. (report)

## relevance

- **DIS / baseline — 4/5:** Strong focus on upcoming-quarter drivers: calendar normalization, segment income requirements, streaming margins, Sports costs, bookings, repurchases and cash flow. Some repeated debates and longer-dated litigation details add limited incremental value to the November earnings setup, but generic company description is largely absent.
  - > How much revenue and operating income does the 53rd week contribute by segment, and does the double-digit FY26 and FY27 adjusted EPS framework include or exclude it? (report)
  - > What is the current state of SVOD ad pricing and sell-through, and does the 13% Q3 margin hold through Q4? (report)
  - > The securities class action trial date moved from August 17, 2027 (10-K) to November 16, 2027 (10-Q), and derivative complaints grew to ten. (report)
  - > For example, we have seen a weaker consumer in Asia in our parks in Shanghai and Hong Kong in Q3, and that's continuing in Q4. (evidence)

## sell_side_usefulness

- **DIS / baseline — 5/5:** Provides a usable modeling framework against available consolidated consensus, calculates prior-year segment comparisons and conditional guidance requirements, identifies accounting and calendar complications, and supplies specific management questions. It clearly discloses unavailable segment consensus rather than inventing it. Surprise thresholds and the guidance items that matter are explicit.
  - > Consensus is $25.48B, implying +13.4% y/y against $22.46B in Q4 FY25. (report)
  - > What does 'growing' mean for Sports in FY26, and what Q4 Sports OI does it imply against $911M last year? (report)
  - > How large is the planned cost reduction in labor and SG&A, when does it land, and how does it relate to the reported Experiences layoffs and project timing? (report)
  - > Whether consensus EPS of $1.70 is adjusted or GAAP, and the FY25 adjusted EPS base behind the 'double-digit adjusted EPS growth' framework, are not in the sources. Segment-level consensus is also absent. (report)

## buy_side_usefulness

- **DIS / baseline — 5/5:** Covers essentially the full available expectations toolkit: consensus and guidance implications, unavailable whisper, implied versus historical moves, beat record, analyst actions, price target, catalysts and risks. Bull/base/bear scenarios provide a clear event setup and distinguish an operating beat from a low-quality EPS beat. The speculative price reactions remain scenarios rather than claimed facts.
  - > The options market implies a +/-7.3% move, against a +/-6.0% average over the last eight reports. DIS has beaten EPS in each of the last four quarters by an average of 5.5%. (report)
  - > The stock is $110.94 against a consensus price target of $125.67 (+13.3%). Recent analyst actions (Citigroup, Needham, Raymond James) all maintained Buy or Outperform ratings. Whisper EPS is not available. (report)
  - > The beat is driven by below-the-line items while the core disappoints. (report)
  - > Options-implied move: ±7.3% (as of 2026-10-30) — EarningsWatcher, https://earnings-watcher.com/wiki/dis-implied-move (evidence)
