## figure_accuracy

- **DIS / baseline — 3/5:** Consensus, segment results, operating KPIs and most derived guidance bridges check out. There are two minor exceptions: annual dividends are incorrectly described as $1.5B rather than $1.50 per share, approximately $2.6B; and $5.56B segment OI does not match the specifically cited F.13 value of $5.55B, although it is defensible rounding of the underlying filing's $5,555M. The checked count covers distinct financial/operating figures and derived calculations, excluding repeated occurrences and calendar dates.
  - > repurchases are running at $7.2B for nine months plus a $1.5B dividend per year. (report)
  - > On November 13, 2025, the Company declared a dividend of $1.50 per share (or approximately $2.6 billion), payable in two semi-annual installments of $0.75 per share on January 15, 2026 and July 22, 2026. (evidence)
  - > Q3 total segment OI was $5.56B (+21.4%), ahead of prior guidance according to management. (report)
  - > [F.13] Operating income, quarter ended 2026-06-27: $5.55B (us-gaap:OperatingIncomeLoss, 10-Q filed 2026-08-05, accession 0001744489-26-000057) (evidence)
- **DIS / pipeline — 5/5:** Source-backed financial and market figures match the evidence. Reproducible calculations—including EPS excluding disclosed items, implied fourth-quarter segment income, extra-week revenue normalization and remaining annual spending—also reconcile at the stated approximate precision. Prospective scenario thresholds are not treated as reported facts or numerical errors.
  - > Nine-month EPS excluding disclosed items is about $5.25 ($4.12 GAAP plus $1.13 of items), against about $4.82 a year earlier ($6.12 less $1.30). (report)
  - > Diluted EPS, nine months ended 2026-06-27: $4.12 (evidence)

  - > Consensus price target: $125.67, +13.3% vs current $110.94 (evidence)
- **DIS / lead — 5/5:** The checkable reported figures, consensus figures, guidance values, peer statistics, and historical-base calculations match the evidence at their stated precision. Assumption-dependent forecasts and scenario thresholds are not treated as source errors. Citation-location deficiencies are assessed separately.
  - > Nine-month operating cash flow was $12.52B, capex $6.78B, FCF $5.74B and buybacks $7.245B. (report)
  - > Free cash flow, nine months ended 2026-06-27: $5.74B (derived: operating cash flow F.17 minus capital expenditure F.19) (evidence)

## reasoning_quality

- **DIS / baseline — 3/5:** Provides useful annual-to-quarter bridges, normalizes disclosed EPS items, and appropriately prefers newer reported legal information. However, it treats an unknown consensus basis as definitely unlike GAAP, assumes without evidence that the extra week has low attendance, and infers large Entertainment growth merely from a low comparison base. Scenario Sports thresholds also fall short of the report's own full-year-guidance bridge. These weaknesses prevent a consistently evidence-supported conclusion.
  - > The consensus basis (adjusted or GAAP) is not stated, so the +132% compares unlike measures. (report)
  - > The extra week falls in a low-attendance period, so it is likely less than proportional, which makes $25.48B achievable rather than a low bar. (report)
  - > Year-ago Q4 Entertainment OI was only about $691M (4,674 - 3,983), so growth should remain large. (report)
  - > The 10-Q is newer and the later date applies. (report)
- **DIS / pipeline — 3/5:** Most conclusions have substantive support, and the report appropriately distinguishes GAAP from adjusted comparisons, qualifies peer inferences, and prioritizes the filing/CFO over an analyst's buyback reference. However, full-year double-digit SVOD margin does not require fourth-quarter margin to remain double-digit. The base case also allows Sports growth below the report's own requirement for full-year growth. Finally, the data-gap assertion that management's promotion explanation is unsupported contradicts the supplied transcript and the report's own discussion.
  - > Management reiterated double-digit SVOD margin for FY26 ex-53rd week, so Q4 should stay at or above double digits. (report)
  - > We delivered a 13% SVOD operating margin in fiscal Q3, and we remain on track for double-digit margins in fiscal '26, excluding the 53rd week impact. (evidence)
  - > Sports OI up but short of the roughly +30% implied (report)
  - > The passages show the analyst's question about the new U.S. discount programs but not management's answer, so the claim that they are targeted and not a health signal is unsupported. (report)
  - > We've been deploying promotional offers regularly. And what they're really about is going after a targeted market segment to drive incremental value and ultimately make sure that we're making the best use of our assets and all the capacity that we have available to us. (evidence)
- **DIS / lead — 2/5:** Several useful bridges distinguish operating performance, calendar effects, and one-offs, and the report correctly prioritizes filing revenue over conflicting news. However, its central EPS setup repeatedly claims that including-week guidance is unavailable even though the supplied article explicitly gives it and the report itself includes it in guidance. This leaves the consensus comparison unnecessarily non-comparable. The inferred Street segment-OI expectation and revenue achievability also depend on assumptions without a sufficient earnings bridge.
  - > The guide including the 53rd week is not in the sources I cite, so I cannot derive a like-for-like comparison or the value of the extra week. (report)
  - > Adjusted EPS growth of approximately 12% in fiscal 2026, excluding the impact of the 53rd week, or approximately 16% including it. (evidence)
  - > Consensus EPS above the guide-implied figure is consistent with the Street at about $5.0B or more, but I found no segment consensus. (report)
  - > Discrepancy on Q3 revenue: the 10-Q shows $25.248B while a Yahoo/Bloomberg article cites $25.17B. I relied on the 10-Q. (report)

## evidence_labelling

- **DIS / baseline — 4/5:** Market estimates are separated from reported results and have providers and as-of dates. Call material is identified as Q3 commentary with its unavailable date disclosed. Own calculations are generally identified. Some interpretation is nevertheless promoted to a sourced fact or management outlook, notably the claimed nonrecurrence of the corporate legal benefit, and some derived conclusions carry reported/guided tags without clearly identifying the unsupported portion.
  - > This is my own arithmetic, not guidance. (report)
  - > The earnings call date is not given, and the call describes the A+E deal as announced overnight while the 10-Q says the agreement was entered in July 2026. (report)
  - > Q3 benefited from about $100M of tariff refunds in Experiences and a favorable legal resolution in corporate expense. Neither is expected to recur at that size. (report)
  - > Corporate and unallocated expenses decreased $76 million for the quarter, from $410 million to $334 million, primarily due to a favorable resolution of a legal matter in the current quarter compared to a legal settlement in the prior-year quarter and timing of allocations to the segments, partially offset by a gain on a land sale in the prior-year quarter. (evidence)
- **DIS / pipeline — 5/5:** Post-filing estimates, peer results and secondary reporting are distinguishable from Disney's filing results and management outlook. Source dates are generally available in the market fields or source map; the unavailable call date is explicitly acknowledged. Own calculations and inferred guidance are qualified in plain language, even where the broad evidence_type tag is imperfect.
  - > Barchart (2026-10-28) describes a third round of layoffs since March 2026 under CEO Josh D'Amaro and a possible television overhaul. (report)
  - > This is an inference, not Disney data. (report)
  - > This is our reading of 'growing', and the guide's exact wording is unavailable. (report)
  - > The call date is not given (it appears to be the Q3 call). (report)
- **DIS / lead — 4/5:** Generally separates historical results, management outlook, analytical derivations, and peer proxies. Market observations and most news have dates and identifiable sources, and unsupported headline commentary is explicitly marked unverifiable. Some post-filing developments lack a clear source in their claim, particularly the FCC lawsuit, while portions of the uncited data-gap commentary are less clearly labelled.
  - > Mid-single-digit FY growth (guide as reported by secondary coverage) (report)
  - > This is my derivation, not a company figure. (report)
  - > Two headline-only items from Oct 29–30 add colour but no numbers. (report)
  - > Disney and ABC later sued the FCC (Aug 18). (report)

## source_traceability

- **DIS / baseline — 4/5:** Nearly all substantive claims have existing evidence IDs and corresponding source-list entries. Most citations contain the relevant facts. A few are incomplete or misplaced: the diluted-share figure cites Q.002 rather than the earnings/share table, content amortization cites Q.103 rather than Q.102 or Q.037, and several conclusions extend beyond their cited passages. Source-list section descriptions are also sometimes inaccurate.
  - > Diluted shares were 1,743M in Q3 and are shrinking with buybacks. (report)
  - > There were 1,726,686,902 shares of common stock outstanding as of July 29, 2026. (evidence)
  - > Nine-month capex was $6.78B (+11%), nine-month repurchases were $7.2B, and nine-month content amortization was $19.3B. (report)
  - > 10-Q filed 2026-08-05 · Financial Statements — Disaggregation of Income Statement Expense (report)
- **DIS / pipeline — 4/5:** Nearly all substantive narrative claims have existing evidence IDs, with locations recorded in the source map. Some citations are incomplete or point to adjacent passages rather than the actual fact: Entertainment's advertising-rate decline is sometimes assigned to Q.081 instead of Q.082, and healthy forward bookings are attributed to C.003 rather than C.004/C.020. The market block's whisper and analyst actions lack corresponding M.02/M.06 entries in the source map.
  - > Management says forward bookings at Walt Disney World and the cruise line are healthy, and that targeted discounts (after-2pm pricing, Anaheim resident pricing) are not a demand warning. (report)
  - > Forward bookings at Walt Disney World and Disney Cruise Line remain healthy. (evidence)

- **DIS / lead — 3/5:** Most factual claims have existing evidence IDs and a corresponding source-register entry. Several important citations do not contain the claimed fact: A.015 and A.021 do not supply the Q4 OI and extra-week contribution, Q.135 does not contain the subsequent lawsuit, and some Q3 consensus comparisons lack the relevant news citation. Numerous data-gap assertions and scenario triggers are also uncited.

## relevance

- **DIS / baseline — 4/5:** Predominantly focused on Q4 drivers, the extra week, segment guidance bridges, earnings quality and FY27 outlook. Some litigation chronology and longer-term platform strategy add detail without a clear near-term earnings transmission mechanism, but they do not dominate the report.
  - > How large is the 53rd-week contribution to Q4 revenue, segment OI and EPS, and how should we adjust the reported guides? (report)
  - > What is the numerical Sports OI guide for FY26, and what drives a return to growth in Q4 after -17% in Q3? (report)
  - > Derivative suits have grown to ten in the 10-Q from five filed plus one received in the 10-K, and the Biddle/Fendelander settlement has preliminary approval with a final hearing on January 14, 2027. (report)
- **DIS / pipeline — 4/5:** The central analysis is tightly focused on the upcoming quarter: the extra week, segment profit requirements, parks demand, streaming economics, Sports rights costs and FY27 expectations. Repetition across sections, broad litigation/IP discussion and lengthy internal check notes add material that does not materially sharpen the earnings setup.
  - > How much do the 53rd week and the cost actions contribute to Q4 revenue, segment OI and EPS, and what does the underlying quarter look like without them? (report)
  - > What is the path to FY26 Sports OI growth after a 14% nine-month decline, and how are the Comcast NFL Network blackout and other renewals affecting affiliate revenue? (report)
  - > Generative AI is flagged as a risk to IP protection, since AI tools have facilitated infringing works and the legal landscape is uncertain. (report)
- **DIS / lead — 4/5:** Strong focus on the upcoming quarter's segment-profit hurdles, extra week, streaming advertising, parks demand, cash conversion, and FY27 outlook. Repeated presentation of the same bridges and some distant litigation and low-information headline commentary dilute an otherwise well-targeted preview.
  - > What drives the roughly +45% Q4 step-up in Sports OI implied by the FY guide? (report)
  - > Q4 SVOD margin and ad revenue growth, Entertainment OI against the implied ~$1.0B ex-week, and any FY27 margin target. (report)
  - > The securities class action alleges misstatements about Disney+ costs and subscriber growth and has a trial date of Nov 16, 2027. (report)

## sell_side_usefulness

- **DIS / baseline — 5/5:** A useful modeling and management-question framework: compares revenue/EPS with consensus, derives quarterly implications of annual guidance, identifies missing segment consensus, and specifies issues that would drive estimate revisions. Questions are company- and quarter-specific rather than generic.
  - > On FY25 OI of $9,995M and nine-month FY26 OI of $8,941M, that implies Q4 (ex-extra week) of roughly $1.85-1.95B, against a derived year-ago Q4 of $1,878M (9,995 - 8,117). (report)
  - > How much of Q3 and Q4 Entertainment OI growth comes from lower marketing spend versus recurring subscription growth, and what is the Q4 SVOD margin? (report)
  - > Consensus basis (adjusted versus GAAP), analyst count and segment-level consensus are not provided; Q4 FY25 adjusted EPS and the FY25 adjusted EPS base for the double-digit growth guide are not in the sources. (report)
- **DIS / pipeline — 5/5:** Provides a usable consensus-relative modeling framework, identifies the implied segment hurdles and the extra-week adjustment, acknowledges missing segment consensus, and poses specific management questions. Bull/base/bear conditions make clear which operating and guidance developments would surprise the Street.
  - > Consensus embeds the extra week. Treating it as roughly 1/14 of the quarter, $25.48B implies about 5% underlying growth, below Q3's 6.8%, which included about 2pts from Fubo and the NFL assets. (report)
  - > How large and how quickly will the labor and SG&A reductions flow through, what charges will they trigger, and is double-digit FY27 EPS growth still the target given consensus of +8%? (report)
  - > No consensus is available for segment operating income (Experiences, Sports, Entertainment), SVOD margin, tax rate, or free cash flow, so those metrics have no consensus line. (report)
- **DIS / lead — 4/5:** Provides actionable model metrics, annual-to-quarter segment bridges, cash-flow requirements, and specific management questions. Explicitly acknowledges unavailable segment consensus. The incomplete like-for-like EPS comparison and speculative Street OI inference prevent the strongest score.
  - > Each point of full-year growth is about $100M of Q4 OI. (report)
  - > Please separate NBA rights timing, the roll-off of UFC costs, NFL and college football phasing, ESPN DTC marketing and the 53rd week. (report)
  - > What conversion of earnings to cash gets Q4 operating cash flow to about $6.5B, and how does the buyback pace beyond the A+E proceeds fit with leverage in FY27? (report)

## buy_side_usefulness

- **DIS / baseline — 5/5:** Includes essentially the full available expectations setup: consensus, explained whisper absence, options versus historical move, beat record, analyst actions, target upside, catalysts, risks and explicit scenarios. It identifies Sports recovery and the Experiences guidance bridge as key debates and gives observable settlement metrics.
  - > Disney beat EPS consensus in each of the last 4 quarters, with an average surprise of +5.5%. The options market implies a ±7.3% move into the print, against a ±6.0% average closing move over the last 8 reports. (report)
  - > The stock is at $110.94 against a consensus price target of $125.67 (+13.3%). Recent rating actions (Citi, Needham, Raymond James) were all maintained Buy/Outperform. (report)
  - > Reported Q4 Sports OI against the roughly $911M year-ago base, and whether management quantifies the 53rd-week contribution. (report)
- **DIS / pipeline — 5/5:** Presents a clear expectations and catalyst setup, including alternative consensus estimates, whisper unavailability, implied versus historical volatility, beat history, analyst positioning and valuation targets. The FY27 consensus-versus-guidance gap and conditional operating scenarios give investors useful criteria for evaluating the release and subsequent stock reaction.
  - > The options market implies ±7.3% against a ±6.0% average historical move. Disney has beaten EPS in 4 of the last 4 quarters (average surprise +5.5%). (report)
  - > FY26 consensus is +16.7%, but FY27 consensus ($7.48) is only +8.1%, below the double-digit target. (report)
  - > Revenue lands above $25.5B and EPS above $1.80. Domestic parks hold attendance +3% and per-capita +4%, and Experiences OI exceeds the implied guide. (report)
- **DIS / lead — 4/5:** Offers a clear event setup, implied-versus-historical volatility comparison, beat record, analyst sentiment, valuation reference, catalysts, and bull/bear triggers. Its usefulness is limited by the unresolved guidance-consensus EPS bridge and scenario thresholds that are not connected through a coherent earnings or valuation model.
  - > The stock is $110.94 against a consensus price target of $125.67 (+13.3%). (report)
  - > The company has beaten EPS in 4 of the last 4 quarters, with an average surprise of +5.5%. (report)
  - > Total segment OI lands near or slightly above the guided ~$4.9B (including the 53rd week) and adjusted EPS comes in around the $1.70 consensus. (report)
