## reasoning_quality

- **AAPL / pipeline — 3/5:** The core analysis is well grounded: it separates refund-assisted earnings from underlying performance, bridges guidance to EPS, distinguishes proxies, and exposes competing consensus estimates and the IBD growth inconsistency. However, calling the difference between undated Finnhub data and dated news a downward consensus drift assumes a chronology the evidence does not establish. The editorial explanation for deleting the Services bear case also contradicts the CFO's explicit discussion of softer-than-expected mobile gaming.
  - > Both consensus sets are estimates, so we show both rather than pick one. (report)
  - > Consensus has drifted down vs the earlier Finnhub figure (report)
  - > The Finnhub figure is undated and above the guide's top end. (report)
  - > The CFO cites mobile gaming headwinds and a June-quarter App Store revenue record, but nowhere says the App Store was 'a bit softer than expected' (report)
  - > I would say that we roughly met our expectations, but we did see a bit more softness on mobile gaming in the App Store. (evidence)
- **AMD / pipeline — 3/5:** The central setup is supported: expectations exceed the revenue midpoint, guidance-derived EPS provides an independent cross-check, and peer results are treated as proxies. EPS-source differences are explicitly acknowledged rather than silently combined. Nevertheless, the report incorrectly claims Q2 non-GAAP EPS is absent from all provided sources, calls mark-to-market income categorically non-recurring, and leaves some conclusions stronger than their support. These issues are distinct from numeric correctness.
  - > This is my arithmetic, not company guidance. (report)
  - > My guide-implied estimate of about $1.93 suggests $1.97 is the better bar, but the basis is not confirmed. (report)
  - > A Q2 non-GAAP EPS figure is not in the provided sources. (report)
  - > Non-GAAP operating income rose 245% to $3.09 billion, while non-GAAP EPS reached $1.66. (evidence)
  - > This is non-recurring mark-to-market income, so GAAP EPS is a poor guide to run-rate earnings. (report)
  - > These investments are subject to market risks that could substantially impact their fair value. (evidence)
- **AMZN / pipeline — 3/5:** The main investment debates are supported, management aspirations are distinguished from formal guidance, and differing consensus benchmarks are explicitly flagged. However, one quarter exceeding guidance is generalized into a record of beating ranges, and the base-case negative free-cash-flow conclusion lacks an operating-cash-flow forecast. These weaknesses concern inference rather than the numeric errors scored separately.
  - > Coverage dated Oct 30 cited a lower ~$244.5B consensus. The two figures differ and the discrepancy is unresolved. (report)
  - > This is framing, not formal guidance. (report)
  - > operating income lands near the midpoint-to-high end ($31–34B, given Amazon's record of beating its range) (report)
  - > cash capex of about $48B implies free cash flow still negative. (report)
  - > Free cash flow is cash flow from operations reduced by “Purchases of property and equipment, net of proceeds from sales and incentives.” (evidence)
