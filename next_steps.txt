9. Improve Retrieval Quality
   WHEN: R1, R2, R3 BEFORE SMOKE (shared inputs and citation labels for
   every arm). R5 CONDITIONAL: BEFORE SMOKE if Rec 2's workers search
   passages (decided when Rec 2 is designed); otherwise it covers only

   comparison would be unfair to Rec 2); otherwise not needed.
     FINDINGS (2026-10-30, offline check of the 26 cached tickers' filings)
   - The bigger problem is section LABELS, not chunk splitting. The
     chunker's section detector mislabels most filings, not just JPM:
     * In most 10-Ks "Quantitative Disclosures" (Item 7A, usually about a
       page) covers 30-50% of the text (AMZN 50%, CAT 49%, JNJ 54%), while
       MD&A gets 2-3% (CAT, KO, MSFT, JPM). In 10-Qs, "Risk Factors" often
       takes 30-60% (AMD 63%, FTNT 59%) and "Other" up to 81% (JPM).
     * Cause 1: a heading is ANY short line that contains a section
       phrase, so a footnote ("(c) ... on the Consolidated balance
       sheets"), a bullet ("• ... Item 1A: Risk Factors in ..."), a
       running page header ("Management's discussion and analysis") or a
       glossary entry counts as a heading.
     * Cause 2: "keep the last occurrence of each label" assumes the table
       of contents comes first and the body heading last. Cross-references,
       footnotes and glossaries after the body break that, so the chosen
       "heading" is often near the end of the filing.
     * Cause 3: only five sections are detected, so a section runs until
       the next detected heading: Item 7A swallows Item 8 and the notes,
       because "Item 8. Financial Statements" is not one of the patterns.
     * Cause 4: some 10-Ks are a short wrapper around an annual report
       (JPM's cross-reference index; USB incorporates its Annual Report,
       Exhibit 13, which Yoda does not read at all: its "10-K" is 138K
       characters with no MD&A or financial statements).
   - Where it matters today:
     * Every filing citation carries the label ("10-K · Risk Factors —
       ..."), so readers (and the judge) see the wrong Item named.
     * The size rule trims an older 10-K by label (Financial Statements,
       then Quantitative Disclosures, then MD&A), so it can cut MD&A text
       labelled as 7A. This only affects JPM-size filings (see below).
     * Retrieval itself ignores labels: chunks are found by embedding
       similarity alone (text-embedding-3-small via ChromaDB). The
       personalities' retrieve_filing returns the top 5 chunks from each
       filing by default (they may ask for up to 10), plus matches from the
       8-K/6-K and the call; lookup_peer returns the top 3 (max 5) from a
       peer's newest filing.
