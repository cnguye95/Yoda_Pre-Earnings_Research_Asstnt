3.2 Evidence types — tag every claim
   reported     = company actuals for a closed period (10-Q/10-K, 8-K 2.02 release)
   guided       = management's own outlook (release, call, 8-K 7.01)
   estimate     = third-party expectation (consensus, analyst targets, whisper)
   proxy        = indirect signal (alt data, peer read-through, industry stats)
   unverifiable = claim lacks evidence to be tagged reported, guided, estimate, or proxy
   - Add evidence_type + as_of_date + source to Hypothesis evidence quotes and
     to a new report section (see 3.5).
   - News articles are tagged by the ORIGINAL source the article names for
     the claim, not as "news". The personality may tag the claim when the
     article attributes it, e.g.:
       "Netflix reported revenue of $X in its Q2 filing"  -> reported
       "management expects Q3 margins of Y%"               -> guided
       "analysts polled by LSEG expect EPS of $Z"          -> estimate
       "card-spending data from <firm> shows sales down"   -> proxy
     The attributed source is recorded alongside the tag.
     * The outlet (e.g. marketbeat, SeekingAlpha) is recorded too: it comes
       from the article URL's domain, so no extra field is needed (AGREED).
   - Edge case: if the article does not say where the claim comes from, the
     claim is tagged "unverifiable".

   - Historical earnings move (AGREED): a new consensus.py function giving

     * A company with no past reports, or data that can't be fetched,
       returns None with a reason — never an error, never 0. The report
       shows N/A.
     * DONE (step 3, 2026-10-28): get_historical_move(ticker) averages the

3.4 Weigh claims — two dimensions: reliability and relevance (AGREED DESIGN)
   - Recommendation (agreed):
     * Reliability: categorical tag (reported > guided > estimate > proxy >
       unverifiable; unverifiable ranks last — AGREED).
       Assigned in code wherever the source type is known (SEC filings,
       consensus feed). For news articles the personality assigns it from
       the source the article names, and code checks the tag fits that
       stated source (3.2); no stated source -> "unverifiable".
     * Relevance: coarse category judged by the personality per hypothesis
       — "direct" (same metric/period as the question) vs "context" — with a
       one-line reason, which the critique step can challenge.
     * No numeric weights at first. Rules instead: a hypothesis needs at
       least one direct claim; proxy-only hypotheses are capped at 3/5;
       unverifiable-only hypotheses are capped at 2/5 (AGREED).
     * Conflict rules (AGREED; the same wording goes in 3.6 and in the
       reasoning_quality anchor, 3.7):
       - For the same metric and period, the more reliable tier wins and
         both are shown.
       - Newer information is preferred only within the same tier, or when
         it covers a later period.
       - Guidance, consensus and whisper for the same period are different
         quantities, not conflicting claims. Showing the gap between them
         is good. The tier rule applies only when sources give different
         values for the same fact.
     * Revisit numeric weighting only if the eval shows the categorical
       rules aren't enough.

3.7 Eval — new rubric and judge (AGREED; yoda/eval/rubric.py, judge.py,
    runner.py). No eval or re-score runs until the rest of next_steps is
    done, and each run needs explicit consent.
   - Rules for every dimension (rubric header):
     * Scores 2 and 4 sit between the neighbouring anchors.
     * Score only from the evidence given; anything not in it is
       unverifiable.
     * Length is not quality: do not reward a longer report for length
       alone.
     * Score each dimension on its own; a strength in one must not raise
       another.
     * Each justification quotes the evidence passages behind the score
       (the quote check, below).
     * Dimensions code marks N/A are not scored.
     * No double counting: numeric correctness is scored only in
       figure_accuracy; source/date labelling only in evidence_labelling
       and source_traceability.
   - Nine dimensions, each scored 1-5 with written 1, 3 and 5 anchors
     (AGREED 2026-10-28; step 7 copies them into judge.py word for word):
     * filing_extraction — material facts in the 10-Q/10-K (key_metrics,
       segments, risks, forward_guidance) and in post-filing 8-Ks
       (since_filing: earnings-release results, guidance updates,
       material events). Company guidance, from filing or 8-K, is scored
       here. Judge materiality, not volume.
       1 = misses most headline figures (revenue, EPS, segment results) or
           the filing's guidance; ignores post-filing 8-Ks that were in the
           evidence.
       3 = headline figures and most segments captured, but misses some
           material items: guidance details, a new or changed risk, or a
           material 8-K event or release figure.
       5 = all headline figures, every reported segment, the filing's
           guidance and new or changed risks, and every material
           post-filing 8-K captured.
       The 8-K part is N/A when the mode had no 8-Ks.
     * market_expectations — third-party expectations only: consensus
       EPS/revenue, whisper, implied move, historical move. Scores coverage
       and the right quarter; the numbers are checked in figure_accuracy.
       1 = no expectation figures, or figures for the wrong quarter.
       3 = consensus EPS and revenue for the right quarter, but whisper,
           implied move or historical move missing although the market
           data had them.
       5 = every item the market data had is present for the right
           quarter; items the data lacked are shown as N/A with the reason,
           not left out or guessed.
       N/A when the mode had no market data.
     * figure_accuracy — "A figure is accurate if it matches the source it
       is attributed to, as that source appears in the evidence. A
       consensus number is checked against consensus, not reported
       results. A figure whose source is not in the evidence is
       unverifiable: list it, and do not count it as an error."
       Rounding that keeps the stated precision ($10.2B for $10,246M)
       matches.
       1 = several figures don't match their source, or a key figure
           (revenue, EPS, guidance, consensus) is wrong.
       3 = key figures match; one or two minor figures are wrong (wrong
           period, unit, or rounding beyond the stated precision).
       5 = every checkable figure matches its source, with the right
           period and unit.
       N/A (set by code) when no figure in the report is checkable against
       the evidence (AGREED); leaning on unverifiable claims is penalized
       under reasoning_quality instead. The judge reports how many figures
       it could check, so code can apply this.
     * reasoning_quality — the three conflict rules in 3.4, word for word;
       contradictions flagged; and "A conclusion resting only on
       unverifiable claims is weak and lowers the score. A conclusion
       supported by at least one verifiable claim (reported, guided,
       estimate or proxy) is more reliable than one supported only by
       unverifiable claims, even if unverifiable claims also support it."
       Judged against the evidence the mode had: a report with one dated
       source can score 5.
       1 = a conclusion contradicts the evidence; a less reliable or older
           figure overrides a more reliable one for the same fact without
           saying so; or key conclusions rest only on unverifiable claims.
       3 = conclusions mostly follow the evidence and the conflict rules,
           but a conflict is left unflagged, a stale figure is used where a
           newer one of the same tier existed, or some conclusions rest
           only on unverifiable claims.
       5 = every conclusion follows from cited evidence; every conflict is
           shown with which source is more reliable; the
           guidance/consensus/whisper gap is treated as a gap, not a
           conflict; no conclusion rests only on unverifiable claims.
     * evidence_labelling — a separate dimension: post-filing information
       is labelled and not mixed into reported figures. Judged
       tag-agnostic — for each post-filing claim, can a reader tell where
       it came from, as of when, and whether it is a reported result,
       management outlook or third-party estimate? Plain wording ("the
       Street expects", "per the 8-K", "according to Reuters") scores the
       same as an evidence_type tag. Tag correctness is checked by 3.10's
       tests, not by the judge.
       1 = post-filing information mixed into the filing's reported
           figures without saying so, or most post-filing claims give no
           source or date.
       3 = post-filing claims kept apart from filing figures, but some lack
           a clear source, date, or kind (reported / outlook / estimate).
       5 = every post-filing claim says where it came from, as of when, and
           its kind; none mixed into the filing's figures; claims with no
           stated source are marked unverifiable.
       N/A only when the mode had no post-filing evidence AND the report
       makes no post-filing claims (post-filing claims with no evidence
       still score 1).
     * source_traceability — every citation resolves to a real location:
       a filing section, "8-K Item 2.02, Exhibit 99.1 — <heading>", a news
       URL, or a consensus.py source + timestamp.
       1 = most citations missing, vague ("Other", "MD&A") or pointing to
           places not in the evidence.
       3 = most citations resolve, but some are missing or too broad to
           find the fact.
       5 = every citation resolves to a real place in the evidence that
           contains the fact.
       An explicit "UNVERIFIED — the cited source was not the filing" label
       counts as a missing citation, which is better than a made-up
       location.
     * relevance — focus on what matters for the upcoming report. Same
       meaning as the old rubric, with sharper anchors (AGREED) because the
       baseline scored 5.0 on all 20 tickers in the last run.
       1 = mostly generic company description or boilerplate not tied to
           the upcoming report.
       3 = on-topic but padded: standard risk-factor boilerplate, stale
           items, or detail that won't affect this quarter's results or
           guidance.
       5 = nearly everything bears on the upcoming report: this quarter's
           drivers, guidance, KPIs, and the risks and developments that
           could move results or the stock; boilerplate left out.
     * sell_side_usefulness — 1 = restates the filing, no
       consensus-relative view; 3 = names the key metrics and guidance
       items but no consensus comparison or questions for management;
       5 = metrics to model vs consensus, specific questions for
       management, which guidance items matter, what would surprise the
       Street.
     * buy_side_usefulness — 1 = no view on expectations;
       3 = covers part of the expectations gap, implied move or catalysts,
       but no clear setup; 5 = whisper vs consensus vs guidance, implied vs
       historical move, beat/miss record, analyst actions and price
       target, catalysts and risks, and a clear setup summary. Comparing
       guidance against consensus is credited here.
   - N/A: code marks a dimension N/A for a mode that never had that kind
     of evidence (e.g. no 8-Ks, no market data). Stored as an empty cell,
     never 0. The paired comparison and the chart drop N/A pairs, and
     summary.md shows how many pairs each dimension compared.
   - Judge input: the full evidence, no 30K-character truncation.
     gpt-6.1-sol takes up to 922K input tokens; prompts over 272K cost 2x
     input / 1.5x output for the whole call. The filing goes first in the
     prompt so both modes' calls for a ticker share the cached part.
     Measured in step 4: JPM's three 8-Ks since its 10-Q add ~155K chars
     (~39K tokens; the 99.2 financial supplement alone is 90K chars), so
     JPM's 10-Q + 8-Ks come to ~250K tokens before the report and news —
     close to the 272K line. Decide in step 7 how to stay under it (e.g.
     only the 8-K documents the report cites).
   - Evidence cache for the judge (eval runs only): every mode returns its
     evidence bundle on every run (already in memory, no extra cost); only
     the eval runner writes it to disk, next to the report JSON. The judge
     reads that bundle, so it sees what the report saw.
     * Contents: external sources only — filings, 8-Ks, news results,
       consensus.py data, transcript chunks. Each item carries its own
       citation (source, URL or accession number, as-of date) so the
       report's citations can be matched to it.
     * News: full article text only for articles the report cites AND a
       personality actually read (read_article); every other news-pool
       item as the full Tavily snippet synthesis saw (AGREED). The bundle
       records which articles were read. Keeps the judge's prompt small
       and free of text the report never relied on.
     * Never Yoda's own hypotheses or critique messages: they are Yoda's
       reasoning, not sources, and would let a claim be "verified" by Yoda
       quoting itself. (They get their own grading in 5.)
     * The report itself carries them too: EarningsReport.hypotheses_explored
       holds the panel's hypotheses, and every archived Yoda report contains
       it, so the old judge saw them. The new judge removes
       hypotheses_explored from the report before scoring. This also lets
       the archive load for re-scoring: 8 archived baseline reports hold
       invented hypotheses that today's schema rejects (see section 4).
   - Reliability checks (AGREED):
     * Quote check: for each dimension the judge quotes the evidence
       passages behind its score, and code checks that each quote really
       appears in the evidence (ignoring spacing). A failed quote flags the
       score — kept, not retried, so a retry can't quietly pick a
       favourable score. summary.md shows failed-quote counts per
       dimension. Adds ~$0.03 per report in output tokens.
     * Judge version: no dated gpt-6.1-sol snapshot can be pinned today
       (OpenAI's model page lists only "gpt-6.1-sol"), so record the
       version OpenAI reports for each call (judge_snapshot column in
       results.csv). summary.md warns if a run mixes versions or differs
       from the run it is compared with. Set reasoning effort explicitly
       (medium, the archived run's default) and record it in the run line.
     * Report length: report_chars column in results.csv; summary.md shows
       the correlation between length and score for each dimension.
     * Repeat scoring: ~6 reports (both modes, 3 tickers) scored a second
       time with the judge cache skipped; the second score is stored
       separately. summary.md shows, per dimension, how often the two
       scores match and their average difference. ~$1-1.5, only with
       explicit consent at run time.
   - Runner: update SCORE_COLS, the paired comparison table, the chart and
     the summary.md headings for the new dimensions. Offline tests go in a
     new tests/test_rubric.py.
   - Validation, each pass only with explicit consent:
     * Re-score a subset of the archived 2026-10-26 reports
       (data/eval/archive_2026-10-26_claude-panel_old-rubric/) and show
       2-3 judge justifications per dimension. The archive predates the
       retrofit and has no since_filing, market_expectations or evidence
       bundle, so it only tests the filing-based dimensions.
     * Then score 2-3 fresh tickers to test the new dimensions.
     * Human hand-scoring, required before the first published result
       (not before development runs): 6-10 reports from both modes, scored
       blind (mode and judge scores hidden), at least usefulness and
       reasoning_quality; check how often you and the judge are within one
       point. ~2-3 hours.
   - Re-run baseline vs Yoda once section 4 is built; expect cost/latency
     increase — measure it.

3.8 Earnings press release + call transcript as context
   - Press release: on EDGAR as 8-K Item 2.02, Exhibit 99.1 — free, covered
     by 3.1. Usually the first "reported" numbers for the quarter and the
     main source of quarterly guidance.
   - Call transcript: NOT on EDGAR. Needs a vendor (FMP/Finnhub transcript
     endpoints — check plan cost and terms). Valuable for guidance wording
     and analyst Q&A; long, so retrieve chunks rather than send in full.

3.9 Personality "analyst playbook" + source vetting (current gap)
   - Today the personality prompts give a lens (3-5 sentences) and tool
     rules, but nothing on what moves a stock on earnings (results vs
     consensus, guidance changes, key KPIs, margins), what news to look
     for, or how to judge a source. The models have general financial
     knowledge but are not told to apply it.
   - Code does no source vetting: search_news (Tavily) has no news-topic,
     date-range or domain filter; the URL-pool check only proves a URL came
     from a search, not that the source is credible.
   - Add: a shared playbook section in _BASE_LOOP_CONTRACT; a source-tier
     list (company primary > data vendors > major financial press >
     aggregators/blogs); Tavily topic="news" + a time window starting at the
     filing date; tag-vs-source validation — a tag must match the source
     the article attributes the claim to (see 3.2); a claim with no
     stated source is tagged "unverifiable".
   - Full-article reading (AGREED; cost accepted for now, may cut later).
     Personalities see Tavily snippets cut to 300 characters, which often
     drops the sentence saying where a claim came from — the attribution
     3.2's tagging depends on.
     * Add a read_article(url) tool (Tavily Extract) returning the cleaned
       article, capped at ~5K tokens (AGREED). A typical extracted article
       is ~2-3K tokens, so ~90% come through whole; the cap only cuts long
       pages such as transcripts posted as articles (15-20K tokens). Only
       URLs already in the news pool are accepted, so the URL-pool check
       still holds. Personalities call it only for articles they want to
       rely on.
     * Estimated +$0.08 per ticker, not yet measured: ~9 reads per run,
       ~3K tokens each on average (Haiku ~$0.01; Tavily Extract ~2 credits
       = ~$0.016 once past the free 1,000 credits/month; synthesis ~$0.05
       for the read articles). Judge: ~+$0.06 per report (cited articles
       only, 3.7). Measure on the first 1-ticker run.
     * Synthesis gets the full text of every article a personality read,
       so the report's writer sees the text its claims rest on (AGREED).
     * Rejected: no cap — ~$0.12 per run typical but ~$0.25-0.30 when long
       pages push a Haiku prompt past 100K tokens (5x price), and can push
       JPM's judge call past 272K tokens (2x price for the whole call).
     * Each read uses one of MAX_TOOL_CALLS (6) and adds seconds against
       WALL_TIMEOUT_SECONDS (45s); raise them if reads crowd out other tools.
     * Rejected: full text on every search result (include_raw_content) —
       est. +$0.25-0.55 per run, pushes Haiku prompts past 100K tokens
       (5x price) and floods synthesis and the judge with articles no one
       cited.

5. Yoda-only process check (LATER, separate from Yoda vs baseline)
   The head-to-head eval grades only the final report, so hypotheses and
   critique are kept out of it (3.7). They are still worth grading on
   their own, to find out whether Yoda's extra steps earn their cost:

     faithfully — not dropping them, not inventing new ones?
   - Never mixed into the Yoda-vs-baseline scores: the baseline has no
     process to grade, and showing the judge Yoda's working would tilt
     the comparison toward Yoda.
